The ClearFrame guide how it works, and how to direct it

ClearFrame turns research reports, podcasts, scripts and briefs into narrated motion-graphics films, drawn natively in Rust. This guide covers the pipeline, the vocabulary and, above all, how to ask for a film worth watching.

  • Last updated: 2026-10-05
  • Covers 0.5.0 (unreleased)
  • 33 blocks · 19 palettes
  • Open creative library · editable storyboards

Technical films and review

Start a PR explainer with new FILM --playbook pr-walkthrough --treatment business. A system is one canvas.props.diagram: components with stable ids (user, service, database, queue, state, external), connectors (dashed, flowing) and groups, changed over time by steps — add, remove, replace … with, set a status, and send a request along the connections — each cued to a spoken word. Beats that share node ids morph, so the system carries across cuts instead of being redrawn. clearframe sketch architecture (or component-change, state-machine) prints an editable starting diagram. Layout follows the connections and adapts to wide and tall frames; connectors take separate ports and runs and detour around components. Diagram contract.

How to ask: “Explain PR 214 from the diff: show the old request path, mark the removed call, add the queue in its place, and send one request through the new path.”

Business frames stay steady: the treatment sets the film camera: "none" (a default for every beat, including ones added later; plates then hold still) and lens.handheld: 0; diagram beats hold still by default. check warns when camera, handheld, plate drift and footage motion stack. Motion explains a change; a reading hold is valid.

Choose background or half-screen B-roll before generation (a diagram can take area on one half). plan lists footage that cannot cover its beat plus offset, beats longer than one generated take, and the same seconds shown twice; clips refuses to pay for such a take, and looping is rejected. Inspect text against moving footage at opening, middle, end and lighting changes, including phone size. Blur alone is insufficient. B-roll review.

Watch, leave notes, let the agent edit. viewer --serve is a page for the people a film is for, not for editors. The home page asks What are we making? (your words, wide or tall, any files) and creates the project with its own OpenCode conversation, which drafts a first cut. A film opens on its picture and a filmstrip of its scenes. Click anywhere on the picture to leave a sticky note on that spot at that moment (it keeps the words on screen under it), or write one for the whole film; Send N notes to the agent sends them as one message. The Agent tab beside the picture holds the conversation: one folded line of steps per request, the reply, approvals for commands and paid sound, Stop, and Undo for the newest request's edits. The agent answers each note it acted on in one line; you say Looks good or Not yet, and approve a version in your own words. New versions appear while the page is open. Opened as a file, the page plays films and keeps notes in the browser, and the notes go out as a message to paste into Claude Code. The studio page · setup in agent-studio.md.

How to ask: pin a note on the chart's label, “this should land on the word ‘half’”, then a second on the closing card, “end on the question, not the logo”, and send both.

01the short version

What it is

ClearFrame is an agent-directed motion-graphics system. A model (usually Claude) reads your material and writes one storyboard.json. The scene engine then draws every frame on the GPU (Skia on Metal) from a compiled scene plan: native stages — actors that keep their identity, packets on links, code edits read from a commit, footage, materials, particles, a moving camera with real motion blur — and the blocks' display type, counting numbers, honest charts, diagrams, hand-drawn canvases and captions, each timed to the words of the narration. Google models supply voice, music, stills and the occasional footage insert, but only when you ask for them and set a budget. It has no browser engine, no timeline editor and no npm runtime dependencies. You direct in words, and the storyboard is the whole film.

Research → explainer

Reports become arguments

ingest --markdown pulls every figure out with its sentence and source, along with the tensions, quotes and chart-ready tables. It also scaffolds a question-led research-digest film. The sweet spot is 60–90 s, which is 12–18 beats.

Podcast → clips

Recordings become social cuts

ingest --audio slices a recording into gapless beats with measured word timings and speaker tags. Vertical clips default to pop captions, so the words stay on screen even when a beat turns into a picture.

Script → film

Narration gets pictures

Bring finished words. Each beat gets one idea, one landing word and its own picture. Recording the whole narration as one continuous take keeps one consistent voice performing from line to line.

It also handles brand spots, data stories, lessons and how-tos, one-world journeys through a process or place, and speech-led kinetic type. Run playbooks for the current starting arcs, including financial mechanisms, scenario comparisons and risk tradeoffs.

What it is not

  • Not a slide-deck maker. If the result looks like slides, the film has failed. critique and the review loop exist to catch that.
  • Not a text-to-video generator. Facts, charts and labels are drawn natively. Generated footage is a sparing insert (the default review threshold is 20% of runtime) with its own audio discarded, and it never carries information.
  • Not a fact-checker. It refuses numbers that have no visible source, but it can't tell whether the source is right. The facts are your responsibility.
  • Not a GUI or a browser renderer. The old HTML/GSAP engine is archived for recovery only, and FFFrames (the renderer before the scene engine) is retired. The scene engine is the only renderer.

02from material to MP4

How it works

Material flows in one direction, from the source to storyboard.json to the frames. Direction flows back through a review loop. You change the film by editing the storyboard, and you change the tool by fixing the engine.

  1. MaterialReports (Markdown, text, HTML, DOCX, RTF, or PDF via pdftotext), recordings with word timestamps, a finished script or a brief. A reference film is optional: reference VIDEO writes REFERENCE.md with its cut rhythm, a keyframe sheet, its palette (with the nearest theme) and its motion energy.
  2. Ingest & directioningest --markdown writes BRIEF.md (figures with their sentences and sources, tensions, questions, quotes, tables) and a storyboard for you to rewrite. ingest --audio imports a recording as beats. new DIR --playbook NAME --treatment ID scaffolds a film from a starting arc and writes DIRECTION.md. The story spine goes at the top of DIRECTION.md: the question, the misconception, the turn and the payoff.
  3. storyboard.jsonThis is the single source. It holds the film settings (palette, motion, text motion, captions, sound, voice) and an ordered list of beats. Each beat is a block with validated props, a vo line and optional layers. It is checked against schema/storyboard.schema.json and per-block validators.
  4. Voice & timingvoice --draft records a free local voice. Without --draft, it calls Gemini TTS, which is paid. align --whisper measures word timings locally for free, align --words imports a word file, and align --transcribe is paid. A beat lasts lead + voice + tail, which defaults to 0.25 s + voice + 0.6 s. Measured timings are tied to the hash of the audio file.
  5. job.json + plan.jsonThe engine turns every spoken cue into a time, schedules staged items so they finish inside their beat, compiles native stages into the scene plan, prepares the media under content-hash names and writes build/native/job.json, build/native/plan.json and build/timing.json.
  6. Scene engineThe Rust engine draws each frame on the GPU as a pure function of the frame number and the prepared inputs: the film's backdrop, texture, lens and chrome, each beat's block (built as a display list of shapes, text and pictures and painted on the GPU), native stages between and over them, and dissolves as two real layers. Footage is decoded by FFmpeg at the size it is drawn. There is no random state and no wall-clock animation, so any frame can be re-rendered exactly, and stills, sheets, previews and the final all call the same frame. Scene engine.
  7. MP4render --draft (or preview) uses a fast encoder, about 3× faster than the final. The final render verifies dimensions, FPS and decoded frames, then writes build/video.mp4 with a provenance file recording input hashes, renderer revision, encoder, audio provenance, output hash and colour space.

The review loop

For full 1080p finals, allow at least five minutes in the calling harness for rendering and audio finishing, or resume the same running process session. Longer films may need more time. On this 8 GB Mac, gate full renders and full test suites with codex-heavy; see film/SETUP.md for the render command.

critique is instant and needs no render. It flags deck-like runs, stillness, text density, weak hooks and "and then" story chains. check validates inputs and native diagnostics, and runs a frame audit that fails on held type cut by the frame edge or the letterbox, type over type or over a 3D subject, and type too small to read. sheet renders a contact sheet at three moments per beat. The draft MP4 is the thing to watch and listen to, and review DIR decodes frames around every cut and word boundary. qa DIR reads the whole encoded film for the bugs a still cannot show: a one-frame pop, a stretch where the picture barely changes under the voice (it measures change per second against the frame a second earlier), a world cut that jumps, an untagged export, loudness off target, a drop heard in the wrong place. It writes a one-frame-per-second timeline.png and a 360 px phone.png. Last comes a fresh reviewer: a separate subagent that did not write the film and judges it against DIRECTION.md.

node engine/cli.mjs new my-film --playbook data-story --treatment editorial
node engine/cli.mjs critique my-film          # instant: rhythm, density, hook, story links
node engine/cli.mjs draft my-film             # critique + draft voice + check + sheet + draft MP4
node engine/cli.mjs align my-film --whisper   # free, local, measured word timings
node engine/cli.mjs render my-film            # final encode

Review & edit: rough cut, notes, candidates

The person's first look is not the finished film. It is a plan they can read (paper DIR: chapters, timecodes, who speaks, the picture each beat is meant to get, the transcript) and then a rough cut: the whole film in motion on the real renderer, made with existing blocks and captions, with pictures that aren't drawn yet declared as placeholder slates (draft DIR --rough). Bespoke drawing and reviewer rounds come after their notes. For a recording, the words stay as recorded unless they ask for cuts.

Every full render saves a revision in review/ with its inputs kept by content, and review/index.html plays it beside the transcript, with a note box that stamps the revision and playhead. A note such as "at 2:13 the diagram is confusing, keep the voice" is anchored to that cut: the beat, the words around the playhead, their time in the recording. After later edits it is found again by content, never by timestamp: current, moved, changed (maybe already fixed), stale, orphaned (its passage was cut) or addressed. An edit becomes a candidate (revise) with a before/after preview of only the stretch it touched, rendered from the full timeline with the film's own mix, and a plain report of what changed in content, what only looks different because of a neighbour or a film setting, and what merely moved. The person accepts, refines or rejects; applied is not accepted, and an agent's one-shot decision is never shown as acceptance.

--rough relaxes only what is declared unfinished (placeholder slates and elements marked unfinished): a clipped source line, label or caption still fails, as do facts, audio and timing checks. "Keep the voice" keeps the recording and allows only the cuts the person asks for. Recorded narration is cut, split and merged from the untouched source recording, with measured timing intact and byte-identical undo. The contract, with every state and limit, is docs/editing.md.

node engine/cli.mjs paper my-podcast                       # the plan to read
node engine/cli.mjs draft my-podcast --rough               # full-length rough cut → r001 + review/index.html
node engine/cli.mjs note my-podcast "the diagram is confusing" --at 2:13 --rev r001 --keep voice --by Ana
node engine/cli.mjs revise my-podcast --note n001          # candidate + before/after passages
node engine/cli.mjs accept my-podcast r002 --note n001 --by Ana --said "yes, clearer"
node engine/cli.mjs cut my-podcast --words "a story for another day" --by Ana   # undo: uncut --cut c001
node engine/cli.mjs runlog my-podcast                      # measured time per command; gaps are not work

03the nouns you'll direct with

Core concepts

Beatsthe unit of a film

A beat is one idea. It has an id, a block, props and usually a vo line. It can also carry style (voice energy), chapter, transition/exit, tone, camera, plate, art, textMotion, heading and timing (lead, tail, hold, duration). Aim for 5–15 spoken words per beat. critique warns above 40.

Blocks & props33 native blocks

The blocks fall into families: story, numbers, charts, decisions, diagrams, text, media, speech, graphics and motion (see the library tables). blocks NAME prints the props and a ready-to-paste example. Headline blocks take emphasis (up to four whole-word phrases in the accent colour), emphasisStyle: "serif" for the italic feeling word, and align: "center".

Establish the picture before the late word. A staged item’s say overrides the block’s land. The pacing check now reads those item times. For beats longer than 8–10 seconds, plan the lead-in and the hold: opening scenery, a slow push, restrained particles, or reviewed stillness. Use at: 0 and enter: "none" for background art that must be present immediately.

Spoken cuesland · say

land, growSay, drawSay, per-item say and bar focus.say take an exact word or phrase from the vo line, or local seconds. So do the canvas fields say, exitSay, keys[].say and along.say. Digits and number words match either way ("18" finds "eighteen"). Reveals start just early so they settle on the word, and counts pre-roll so the figure finishes as it is said. A missing cue fails, and so does a cue too late to finish its entrance.

Canvas & artdraw what blocks can't

Declare fills on outlines. Stroked circles, ellipses and rectangles still default to a solid accent fill. Set fill: "none" for contour rings and wireframes, or an explicit colour for a filled shape. The checks warn about omissions in canvas and nested art.

canvas is a beat you draw. It offers 13 element types with entrances, keyframes, motion along paths, loops and exits, all cueable to speech. Any beat can also carry art.under / art.over layers made of the same elements, placed in frame pixels: an arrow onto a bar, a circle round a number. Start from a sketch and place things with still --grid. view: "auto" fits a drawing into the space below the title.

Native stagesidentities that move

A stage is drawn by the engine itself on the GPU. Say what happens and the recipe places it: actors (a card per component, its id is its identity), links that follow them, events — send a packet along a link, state, pulse, move, callout, burst, camera — each cued to a spoken word. code: {commit, file, window} shows an edit read from git with lines that keep their place. Elements use the canvas vocabulary plus video, shader, material, connector, attach and large particles; shutter adds real motion blur. Use a stage block for the whole picture, a beat stage under or over any block, or film stages that keep the same actors across several beats. Stages.

Worldsone drawing, a moving camera

Consecutive canvas beats that share props.world form one continuous drawing. Each beat draws only what is new, and everything drawn earlier stays (still looping). The cuts between them are invisible, and the camera travels between camera rects view: [x, y, w, h]. End on a wide view of the whole world. A world survives an interruption: a world beat after a kinetic card resumes where the camera left off. world DIR renders the map with every beat's camera rect numbered.

{ "id": "source", "block": "canvas", "props": { "world": "water", "view": [0, 0, 1920, 1080], "elements": [ …mountains, rain… ] } },
{ "id": "plant",  "block": "canvas", "props": { "world": "water", "view": [1500, 0, 1920, 1080], "elements": [ …the pipe, the plant… ] } },
{ "id": "whole",  "block": "canvas", "props": { "world": "water", "view": [-100, -560, 5200, 2925], "elements": [ …one line of type… ] } }

Treatmentsart direction in one word

A treatment sets the palette, type voice (film.type: the display family and emphasis for titles, statements, endcards, chapters and kinetic text; types lists inter, didone, wide, geometric, condensed, bookish, typewriter), backdrop, texture, frame, motion, transition grammar, voice style and sound, plus some beat defaults. It also writes its rules into DIRECTION.md. Each is a file in library/treatments/, and a project can add its own. Built-ins include editorial, noir, kinetic, sketchbook, blueprint, audiogram, brand, calm, mosaic, tech, monolith, atelier and the print and craft looks (pulp, woodblock, constructivist, deco, handheld, sampler, gallery). Apply one with new DIR --playbook NAME --treatment ID, then adapt it. It is a starting direction, not a limit.

Palettescontrast-checked on load

Every palette has the tokens bg, surface, ink, muted, accent, accent2, positive and negative. You can override any of them with hex, for example "theme": {"base": "ink", "accent": "#d6acff"}, and check warns if the override drops contrast. Canvas colours are tokens too, so art follows the palette and a beat's tone. looks DIR --beat ID renders one frame in every palette.

Motion & text motionhow things arrive

motion.preset is gentle, snappy or spring, and intensity (0–1) scales travel. Values never overshoot. textMotion controls how display type arrives: lines (the default), words, letters or cascade. Use one mode for the film and break it for one or two moments. Scene headings always rise as a line. heading: "bottom" sets a scene's title as a lower third.

Transitionscuts with meaning

The entrances are cut, fade, rise, wipe, push and zoom, plus three graphic ones, panel, iris and whip, that carry one movement across the cut. Exits mirror the next entrance automatically, and scenes never cross-dissolve. Graphic transitions belong at turns in the story. Use cut for rhythm and push for sequence.

Captionsplate · pop · kinetic

captions: true burns phrase captions onto a soft plate, and "auto" turns them on for vertical formats. "pop" gives social captions: heavy outlined phrases of three or four words, with the spoken word on a tilted accent pill. The kinetic block makes speech itself the picture, in highlight, reveal, word or stack mode. Final captioned or kinetic renders need measured word timings.

Sources & honestynon-negotiable

Every displayed number needs a visible props.source and an entry in storyboard.sources, or check refuses it. That covers charts, stats and canvas counts. Record the claim, the source and the as-of date. Illustrative figures must be labelled as such on screen. Sample figures and fictional quotes are never evidence. Generated imagery is illustration, never documentation. Never imitate real people, logos or documents, and disclose synthetic voices.

04what actually moved quality

Getting great outcomes

These are the principles that turned narrated slides into designed films. Each comes with the prop or command that puts it into practice.

  1. Films, not slides.The tells are every beat sharing a layout, a heading over a static graphic, the same fade on every cut, nothing moving once it has entered, and no imagery. Vary the frame: a full-bleed plate, a tone colour block, a kinetic stack, a centred statement, a lower-third heading, or no heading at all. critique counts heading-over-graphic scenes and flags three scenes of the same family in a row.
  2. Story before pictures.Question → misconception → turn → payoff. Choose 3–5 claims and send everything else to the source card. Link beats with but or therefore, never "and then". Read the vo lines alone: they should make an argument you could follow with your eyes closed.
  3. One picture per idea.Choose the picture by what the sentence does, not by what it is about (docs/ideas.md). When the narration says how, why, through, between, until or instead, draw the mechanism rather than the words.
  4. Land on the word.Each beat has one landing word (two at most), the stressed word at the end of the sentence. Cue the visual to it with land or say. Counts finish as the number is said, and world cameras arrive by the new line's first word.
  5. Travel instead of cutting.A process, a place, a chain of causes or a system should be one world with a travelling camera, not five separate drawings. Lay stations out in story order so the camera never re-crosses old material, and finish with a pull-back to the opening shot, changed.
  6. Punctuate rarely.Use one or two tone scenes per minute and graphic transitions only at turns. Everything else cuts, pushes or fades. critique warns when punctuation stops meaning anything.
  7. Something always breathes.Keep something moving: camera drift, a loop on the subject, a drifting plate, ambient art.under, a particles field. Hold still only on purpose.
  8. Look, then ask a fresh reviewer.Diagnostics can't judge taste. Open the sheet, watch the draft, then have a reviewer with no authorship bias judge it against DIRECTION.md. Keep the same reviewer across rounds so it can say which notes are resolved. Sort its notes into tool problems (fix the engine and every film benefits) and film choices (fix the storyboard).
Per minute of film, aim for
  • at least one drawn mechanism (canvas), one colour-block punctuation, and one or two graphic transitions at turns;
  • imagery (plates) where the world matters, and kinetic type where the words are the picture;
  • a hook that lands inside 3 s, about 3 on-screen words per second, and narration at 140–160 wpm.

Shots, not slides: the cinema score

A slide holds a layout until the presenter moves on. A shot is a moment in continuous time, with a camera that has a point of view. critique scores every storyboard out of 100 against the eight tells of a slideshow, and each tell it finds names the fix. The full grammar is in docs/cinema.md, and docs/scorecard.md tracks how close the defaults get.

Film choices that moved it

  • Stations laid out in story order: the sky up, the roof in close, the street plan down, the survey beside it.
  • The world changes state (a day sky, a roof filling with heat) instead of cutting to a new picture.
  • One interruption, at the turn, and the world resumes afterwards.
  • Labels leave at an exitAt after their beat, tidying the world while the camera is away.
  • A callback to the opening number at the end.

Tool problems fixed in the engine (0.5.0)

  • Camera moves landed late. They now pre-roll into the outgoing beat's settled tail and arrive by the new line's first word.
  • dur: 0 keys caused single-frame flashes. Zero-length keys now apply on their own frame.
  • A world lost its place after a card. It now resumes from the camera's last view.
  • Word-by-word headings read as speech-synced. Headings now always rise as a line.
  • Holds froze once the camera arrived. viewDrift now keeps a world alive.

Prompting guide

The model does the authoring. Your job is to hand over material, intent and constraints, and then to direct the review rounds. The fewer creative decisions you leave to guesswork, the better the film.

Asking for a style

Name a treatment when one fits, and say what to keep from it. When nothing fits, describe the look and ask the model to map it to primitives and tell you the mapping before it builds. That keeps the result honest about what the renderer can actually do.

Iteration prompts

Most of the quality comes from rounds. Keep each round specific, and ask for the notes to be sorted into tool problems and film choices.

Voice with Gemini 3.8 TTS

The rule: one continuous take, one short style. Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS keeps one consistent, lively voice when it reads the whole narration in a single request, as one text with one style. Voices drift when lines are recorded separately, each with its own style or inflection, and stitched together: every style re-prompts the voice and every join resets its pitch and energy. ClearFrame therefore records the whole film as one take by default (voice.takes: "film") and cuts it back into beats with measured word timings. Per-beat style is ignored in a continuous take.

08when something's off

Troubleshooting & FAQ

"Heavy-job lock is held by …; the build starts when it finishes."

Native compilation uses the machine-wide codex-heavy lock with one Cargo job. The retained pipeline, sculpture and verification harnesses also enter or inherit that gate. Wrap other full renders and test suites on this small machine; focused checks and editing can proceed. The build names the holder and waits, so let it finish rather than killing someone else's job. Builds are skipped when the renderer source hasn't changed, and the warm cache makes the rest quick. When parallel agents are working, avoid starting concurrent heavy builds.

"Changes outside note n00N's scope"

revise only accepts edits inside the beats the note is about (found again by content in the working copy). Revert the stray change, or widen the scope with --scope ID,ID --reason "…" and tell the person why. Beats that only moved, or that look different because a neighbour changed, are allowed and listed.

A note is "stale" or "orphaned"

Its moment can't be found honestly in the current cut: the quoted words changed, appear twice, or were cut. It is never moved to whatever now plays at that time. Show the person the old moment (its revision, time and words, or page DIR --rev rNNN) and ask whether it still applies.

"cut --note" refuses: the note is orphaned, stale or already addressed

cut --note and cut --at … --rev cut the exact words that were pointed at, found by their identity in the recording's transcript, never by searching for the same text. If those words are gone (cut earlier, or the note's passage was removed), only partly there, or already cut for that note, nothing is cut. Show the person the old moment and ask; to cut other words, name them with --words and --beat.

Final stays open after the person accepted a fix

Final closes only on a decision about the whole current cut and its final encode: accept DIR rNNN --checkpoint final --by NAME --said "…" after rendering the final. Accepting one note's result or some beats, or an earlier cut, does not approve the film; any change to its content (an audio-only one too), a draft rendered over build/video.mp4, or a later rejection reopens it.

restore says the result is "NOT the same" as the revision

A whole restore writes the revision's files, sets aside later library overrides and shadowing media under review/aside/, then compares the working copy with the revision. "Inputs" means the files match but the renderer or fonts changed since, so frames may differ. "NOT the same" lists what still differs (often a shared library from CLEARFRAME_LIBRARY, which lives outside the project); the command exits 1, and restore DIR with the reported restore point undoes it.

The rough cut still fails the frame audit

--rough relaxes only text that is declared unfinished: a placeholder slate, or an element marked unfinished. A clipped source line, a covered label or a too-small caption is still an error, because roughness says nothing about whether text that informs is readable. Fix it, or, if it really is a stand-in, mark that element unfinished (a final render will refuse it until it is finished).

"uses rough, which the canvas block draws"

A stage element uses a canvas-only feature (rough, print, mosaic, morph, solid, meter, a level loop). Move that element into a canvas beat or art, or remove the feature. Nothing is dropped silently.

"Native build needs N GiB free"

The build refuses to start below 10 GiB free with a warm cache, or 25 GiB for a cold build. doctor reports the same thresholds. Free up space rather than deleting the warm cache: a cold rebuild needs far more.

The draft passed, but the final fails on word timing

Drafts accept estimated narration and word timing. Final kinetic or captioned scenes need measured timestamps tied to the current audio's hash. Run align DIR --whisper (free; install with brew install openai-whisper or pip install openai-whisper), import a word file with align --words, or use the paid align --transcribe. Replacing the audio invalidates the alignment. Interpolated words are never labelled measured. build/timing.json shows each beat's wordTiming and alignmentIssue.

"Spoken cue not found" or "cued too late"

Cues must be words exactly as they appear in the vo line. Digits and number words match either way ("18" ↔ "eighteen"). A cue has to leave room for the entrance to finish inside the beat, so cue an earlier word or give the beat more tail.

"Text reaches outside this beat's camera view" / "renders at about N px"

In worlds, check expects the camera to crop the drawing and ignores "cut off by the canvas edge" while the camera is moving. It still warns when a beat's own text falls outside its view, and when text shrinks below about 22 px through the camera's zoom (the warning gives the minimum size in world units). Fix it by widening or moving the view, enlarging the label, or moving the label into the shot. For vertical cuts, set viewTall. Confirm with world DIR and still --grid.

Why won't it render my number?

Every displayed number (stats, charts, canvas count) needs a visible props.source and a sources entry, and check refuses it otherwise. Professional audiences forgive plain design but not a wrong number, and the storyboard plus its sources is the audit trail. Illustrative figures must say so on screen. Also make sure the number spoken, the number shown and the number in sources agree.

"Beat ends before its counters finish"

A final render fails (and a draft warns) when a count or bar hasn't reached its true value by the end of the beat. Raise the beat's tail or hold, or cue the count earlier.

A graphic transition became a fade

The outgoing scene had no room to finish the cover, so check warns and falls back to a fade. Give that beat more tail, or use a cut. A related warning: the last element landing less than 0.45 s before the cut. Cue it earlier or extend the beat.

My morph doesn't show

A morph pairs the same element id across consecutive canvas beats. Any transition other than cut hides it, and beats with different views make positions jump.

Text overflows, or a character fails

Text that still doesn't fit its box at 14 px fails. Shorten it or split the beat. The bundled fonts cover Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, arrows, maths and currency, and check names any other character (for example CJK) along with its prop.

Check passes, but it still looks like slides

That's expected. Diagnostics can't judge composition or taste. Run critique, open the sheet, and ask for a fresh review round (see iteration prompts).

A paid command asks for a key, or I don't want to spend

Paid generation needs GEMINI_API_KEY. Rendering, draft voice, draft music, word-file imports and Whisper alignment are all free. Run plan first, and use --budget and --only to scope a paid call. The budget check is an estimate, not a provider billing cap.

Render refuses to write my --out file

Overwriting an existing explicit --out needs --force.

How long, how many beats, how much footage?

60–90 s is about 12–18 beats, with beats of 3–5 s for professional pieces and 2–3 s for social cuts. Narration runs at about 150 wpm. Generated footage stays sparing: the default review threshold is 20% of runtime, clip audio is discarded, and both joins get reviewed (docs/continuity.md).

A simpler studio: notes and an agent. The scene engine. Every film now renders with ClearFrame's own GPU scene engine (Skia on Metal): a compiled scene plan evaluated frame by frame, with the backdrop, texture, lens, chrome and dissolves composited natively and every block drawn by the engine itself. Native stages add actors that keep their identity, links, packets, callouts, code edits read from a commit, footage as GPU textures, SkSL materials, particles, a 2.5D camera and real motion blur. All 45 playbooks render through it. FFFrames is retired: no FFFrames crate is a dependency, there is no --engine option, and footage is decoded through the ffmpeg/ffprobe tools (bounded, exact final samples, any seek order). Keeping this guide current

This page is meant to change with the project. When a feature lands:

  1. Read the newest CHANGELOG.md section and update What's new. Once a version is released, fold its bullets into one line and start a new list.
  2. Re-run the cheap listing commands (help, treatments, playbooks, themes --json, blocks, motions, sketch) and reconcile the vocabulary tables and counts in the header. New palettes, treatments, sketches and playbooks are files in library/, so git log -- library shows what arrived.
  3. Check new props against schema/storyboard.schema.json and film/canvas.mjs before listing them. Don't document anything you haven't verified.
  4. If a new workflow changes how films are made, add a prompt template or an iteration prompt as well.
  5. Update the Last updated line in the header and the date in the comment at the top of the file.

Structure: one <section id> per topic, in TOC order, with stable ids. Prompt blocks are pre.prompt inside figure.prompt-card, and copy buttons attach automatically. The file is self-contained apart from Google Fonts, and it falls back to system fonts offline.
