## Components and native capabilities

No third-party component source was imported. Candidates from fframes-components, Snapcn, Awesome AI Motion and ThreeUI still require component-level provenance and a ClearFrame use case before any import.

The transferable storytelling ideas now have original native implementations and four new editable playbooks:

- `cost-breakdown`: a whole, one part opened, and a return, with reconciled quantities.
- `where-work-waits`: a computed serial queue and a capacity intervention that carries existing work forward.
- `which-route`: a stated rule, a case, its counterfactual and a return.
- `audience-views`: one table, fixed cells and different audiences.

Named moments and target framing compose with these pictures. They do not constrain a film to a template. Difficult landscape/portrait variants, captions, numeric changes and named refusals live in `test/fixtures/library-bar/`; outputs stay outside the source library. The original `pipeline` sketch is not evidence of a queue simulation, and a generic morph is not evidence of a reconciled drill-down.

The reference standard is readable hierarchy, proportional quantities, aligned data and restrained emphasis, informed by the FT visual vocabulary and Carbon's table anatomy. Mechanical evidence, agent visual inspection and a person's acceptance are different records. Dense table overviews need readable close-ups; a quiet audit does not make their smallest text phone-legible. See `docs/library-bar.md` and the feature guides for supported shapes and refusal boundaries.
