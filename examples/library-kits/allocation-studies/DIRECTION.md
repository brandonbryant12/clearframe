# Allocation trays

**Question.** How can a fixed pool support a different plan without inventing more resources?
**Tension.** A growing destination can look like newly created money or capacity if the source and transit state disappear.
**Turn.** One equal-value tile leaves its source, travels through an empty lane, and enters its destination. The unchanged total stays visible.
**Payoff.** Exact before/after bars show the completed reassignment, including an unchanged destination after a declared round trip.

Three directions were considered: a conventional before/after bar cut (clear totals but no transfer mechanism), a physical ceramic tray scene (tactile but perspective complicates counting), and a native token transfer board followed by a count chart. The third is selected: every tile stays the same size and identity, and the visible parked-plus-transit ledger explains the temporary gap. This establishes I04. It does not claim the separately proposed B13 Blender mosaic is built.

The library's `cash-flow` playbook, `pipeline` sketch, native canvas and editorial treatment were inspected. Their source-to-destination staging informs the sequence, but the quantitative tokens and conservative routing are original. Use paper, geometric native type, blue tiles and quiet tray boundaries. There is no generated media, audio or paid call.

The household case reallocates four 100 USD tiles into a buffer. The work-hour case reallocates two-hour tiles, including two that temporarily leave Quality and return. The second case distinguishes gross transfers from the ending assignment; equal work hours are not equal output. Both are fictional plans, not actual payments, staffing records, forecasts or recommendations.

Landscape arranges trays side by side with a common lower travel lane. Portrait stacks trays and uses a separate right-hand lane. The bottom occupied slot leaves and the next free slot receives, avoiding hidden token reshuffles. Orthogonal segments use linear translation without scaling, rotation, fading or token duplication. The clock is editorial, not observed transfer time. Counts debit at departure and credit at arrival; in-transit ownership is explicitly separate from a parked allocation.

All tokens remain visible from frame zero. Initial and final reading holds are deliberate. The second beat uses the same total count as its fixed scale; shares rounded to one decimal are marked approximate. Quantities are neither animated from invented values nor converted into equal-risk or equal-benefit claims. No optimizer, return, inflow, loss, spending, productivity or protection model is implied.
