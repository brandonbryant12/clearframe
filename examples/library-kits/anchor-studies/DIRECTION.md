# Anchors that survive reuse

Question: where can a native label attach to a prepared object without guessing its image position or covering the subject?

Use two different existing mechanisms: a reservoir whose gate and levels have settled, and a conveyor whose alternate route has finished moving. Each has a separately fitted landscape and vertical source. Preserve their original qualitative meaning. Exact account balances, liquid volumes and throughput are not inferred from their geometry.

Alternatives considered: arbitrary visual label placement, continuously tracked labels, or a declared static reading hold with source-bound projected attachment points and reserved text regions. Choose the third for this first anchor contract. It checks every frame in the claimed range and fails if geometry moves. Continuous tracking and visibility are separate future capabilities.

Reopen the retained original Blender scene with script execution disabled. Project evaluated subject geometry and named local points through its saved camera. Retain top-left normalized coordinates, conservative subject boxes, source scene/clip hashes, exact zero-based frame range and declared text rectangles. Exclude shadows and depth-of-field blur from geometric safety claims. Inspect actual encoded callout placement before delivery.

Use the original research-paper materials, fixed cameras and warm accent. Let the mechanism act first; add sparse native explanation only in its settled hold. A label lives inside a checked copy region and a restrained native leader reaches a named attachment point. Keep a large source/qualification footer and separate portrait composition. Change the copy for two substantive uses of each mechanism to demonstrate that its Blender scene need not rerender.

The first kit remains a prototype until all calculation/geometry checks, encoded placement checks and independent sampled review are retained. Continuous subjective playback and final-master acceptance remain separate. No paid media or voice generation is needed. The goal remains the complete reusable library, including moving-camera and tracked-label work beyond this static foundation.
