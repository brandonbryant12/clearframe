# Camera studies: authored point of view

Question: how can a reusable camera move change what a viewer understands without turning perspective into a quantity encoding?

Tension: a detail, a selected member or a sharp foreground object can hide relevant context. The camera reveals the surrounding system, larger field or deeper layer. The payoff is a composed view with a deliberate reading hold and editable native qualification.

Three different operations form this kit. A detail-to-system perspective dolly starts at a control junction, then reveals connected branches. A hero-to-field pullback changes apparent scale while preserving the selected marker's identity. A fixed camera shifts its focal plane between a near surface and a deeper structure. The first two move the camera; the third moves only focus. These are standalone reusable camera recipes, not arbitrary ambient drift.

Alternatives considered: a flat native crop journey; a filmed-scale procedural stage with physical parallax; and a cut between independently framed stills. Choose procedural perspective for the two context reveals and fixed optical framing for the focus study. This directly exercises the missing Blender camera presets. Native world, dolly and focus tools remain separate existing capabilities; this milestone does not replace them.

The manifold and field have a close opening, one continuous pullback, then a wider held ending. The focus study holds the same frame while attention changes. Start/end poses, target points, lens, shift, focus distance and aperture are declared and keyed at every source frame. A path can be replayed out of order. Geometry and light remain static so the camera's explanatory work is inspectable. Named source phases provide native cue times.

Use mineral and ceramic surfaces, blue structure, amber selection and graphite fittings. No text or numbers are baked into the scene. Native subject labels, one phase caption and limitations stay crisp. The wider system is deliberately cropped in close openings; its final subject bounds must fit the reserved region. The hero remains visible throughout the move. Portrait gets independently fitted start/end views, not a crop of the landscape output. Focus must change visibly in each format, and no native risk/source label may blur with it.

Each operation is shown with two different explanatory subjects: shared infrastructure and an approval network; locations and a specimen field; surface inspection and research context. These are original illustrative analogies, not simulations, observations or independent physical models. Marker count, pipe dimensions, apparent object size, depth and transit time do not encode evidence.

Review opening/action/settlement/hold frames, mid-move occlusion, hero visibility, focus ordering, source placement, phone legibility, optical holds and saved-scene replay. A source/native final hold uses the source's final decoded YUV frame and a lossless static clip so there is no exhausted-media behavior or alternate image decoding path. No narration, music or paid media is needed for these library fixtures.

Keep prototype status until continuous subjective playback and final master review have been completed. Sampled frames, pose math and camera bounds are evidence for these fixtures, not a universal collision/occlusion solver or a claim of film acceptance.

Review-driven revision: the initial f/0.20 focus clip read too subtly at phone size. The final candidate uses an exaggerated f/0.10 treatment, approved in endpoint still review before motion regeneration. This is a stylized attention cue, not a practical lens specification.
