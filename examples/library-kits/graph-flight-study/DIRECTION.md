# Through the time axis — graph-flight candidate

**Question.** What does change over time feel like from inside the chart?
**Tension.** An outside overview makes the comparison clear, but hides the spatial rhythm of successive observations.
**Turn.** Travel along time, changing sides between bars as they rise; then pull out to see the complete series.
**Payoff.** The viewer connects the camera journey to a readable, fixed-scale graph.

User direction, October 3: view the graph from the time-axis perspective, zipping in front of and behind its rising bars. Develop several examples of such Blender scenes and reusable camera tricks within this library run. This takes priority over the allocation-tray visual study. The earlier feedback that details matter remains binding.

## Selected picture

Three options: a fixed oblique reveal; a straight time-axis dolly; or a bounded weave through the gaps, followed by a lifting overview. Select the third. Motion has an explanatory route, not an unrelated orbit. The path must cross the bar plane only inside gaps and maintain clearance from the actual rising geometry.

Satin blue ceramic bars, precise small chamfers, a matte pale plotting surface and fine graphite construction lines. The graph is the environment. No pedestal, generic sculpture, baked labels, floating decoration or unexplained metallic hardware. Controlled side lighting should distinguish the front, flank and top faces, reveal contact, and preserve the silhouette of overlapping bars. No depth-of-field blur on the only information needed to follow the scene.

Bar height is exactly proportional to original fictional values on a common zero baseline. During the reveal, height is a declared animation fraction of the final observation, not an intermediate observation. Perspective appearance is not a common screen-space scale; current date/value and the eventual native overview must remain explicit. The initial visual candidate contains no numerical claim text yet and is not a finished explanatory film.

## User clarification: a 2D chart grid

The user clarified that the bars must be on a grid, like a 2D chart. The chart therefore occupies one upright time/value plane (world X/Z), with thin filled bar marks and horizontal value grid lines. Remove the ground-plane chart construction and architectural-volume interpretation. The camera creates depth by travelling along and crossing the chart plane; the data remains a flat chart. Keep a shared zero baseline. End facing the chart squarely. Earlier floor-grid stills are superseded visual tests, not the requested direction.

## Full-chart ending

User requested that the ending zoom out to reveal the full chart. The revised draft uses a two-second camera pullback to a centered frontal overview, then a 2.5-second stationary reading hold. The complete baseline, value axis, grid and all eight bars must fit with visible outer margins. Camera distance increases from 36 to 40 world units; the flight remains unchanged. Total draft duration is nine seconds. This is a framing and pacing revision, not quantitative asset acceptance.

## Human pacing correction

The user found the camera too fast. Supersede the four-second flight with ten seconds, increase the pullback to three seconds, and keep a 2.5-second full-chart hold. The new source pass is sixteen seconds at 24 fps; it renders new intermediate poses instead of slowing an encoded clip by repeating frames.
