# Shared scales, different explanations

## Intent

Build a reusable quantitative picture, demonstrated by two subjects. One separates money added from total account balance. The other shows why missing observations must remain missing. These are individual library specimens, not a finished narrated film.

## Look and motion

Warm paper, geometric display type and sparse rules. Colored lines do the explanatory work. Exact observations appear on a shared, linear reveal clock. Reserve the right edge for direct series names and final values. Keep labels apart without moving the data. Show source and as-of date, with a plain missing-data note where needed. No decorative 3D, camera drift, generated footage or audio is needed for these quantitative specimens.

Use the entire width for landscape comparison. In vertical output, retain a taller plotting region and a narrower label column, with ample space below the x axis and above the source. Review long labels and phone-size text; do not accept a scaled landscape crop as the portrait design.

## Truth

Every input is fictional. The retirement example is a flat 6% hypothetical annual return with end-of-year employee/employer contributions, no fees and no withdrawals. It is not a promised outcome or a representation of a real plan. Native values use thousands of USD. The retained ledger gives the unrounded calculation. The sensor example uses deliberately uneven dates and a null reading to test both calendar spacing and gaps.

## Review questions

- Can a viewer identify contributions versus growth before reading an explanation?
- Does the line reveal reach the same date for both series, even with different sample intervals?
- Is there a genuine gap at a missing observation, including on decoded intermediate frames?
- Can final values and source labels be read at phone size?
- Are labels, grid and leaders unambiguous when endpoints nearly coincide?

## Decisions

Use source validation, a contact sheet, decoded encoded-motion frames and phone-size output before assigning readiness. No paid generation. No final-asset acceptance until independent review resolves substantive findings. Draft silent clips are intentional library specimens; they do not establish speech synchronization.

The 10-second and 13-second beats intentionally hold the final quantitative picture after its four-second reveal. Critique's static-hold notices are accepted for reading the axes, final values and missing-data note. Do not add arbitrary drifting geometry to suppress them.
