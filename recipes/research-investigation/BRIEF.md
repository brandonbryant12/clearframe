# Investigate a report through its evidence and its missing test

Audience: People who need to understand how strong a research claim actually is

Inputs: A research brief, exact claims and citations, methods, limitations and the comparison that could change the conclusion

An editorial investigation, not a report read aloud. Start with a live question; separate what the source observed from what the author infers; cut to the missing comparison; narrow the conclusion; hold before the final test. The manuscript drawings are symbolic props, not facsimiles of a real publication. Replace the hypothetical waiting-time example with exact source claims and citations. Preserve methods, counterevidence and uncertainty. Use a sourced quantitative chart only when the source supplies comparable numbers; these drawings do not encode effect size. The page cuts and annotations carry the edit rather than a tour through a diagram world. Pair with the editorial treatment. See docs/source-playbooks.md.
